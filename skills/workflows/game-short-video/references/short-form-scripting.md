# Short-form scripting for a 9:16 game short

The frame is 1080×1920 and the viewer is one thumb away from leaving. Structure carries the
video; the art only stops the scroll.

## Length and density

| Target length | Scenes | Spoken words | Words per scene |
|---------------|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| 30 s | 6–8 | 60–80 | 8–11 |
| 45 s | 8–10 | 90–115 | 9–12 |
| 60 s | 10–12 | 120–150 | 10–13 |

At ~2.6 words per second of Indonesian or English narration, those numbers hold. More words per
scene than the table means the caption cannot be read in time, which is the failure mode that
kills retention even when the visuals are good.

## The four beats

1. **Hook (0–3 s).** A claim, not a greeting. Name the thing the viewer already suspects.
   - Weak: "Hi guys, today I want to show my game."
   - Strong: "This enemy learns your dodge pattern. That's the whole game."
2. **Ground (3–10 s).** One concrete image that proves the claim. No lore, no feature list.
3. **Turn (10–35 s).** One mechanism or one change, explained through a single analogy the
   visuals can show. If the script needs a second idea, it is a second video.
4. **Loop (last 2 s).** A closing line that returns to the hook, so a replay feels intentional.

A 45-second short that carries one idea beats a 60-second short that carries three.

## Captions are the script, not a transcript

- Two lines maximum, ~26 characters per line at 1080 wide.
- Keep the caption and the narration different registers: the caption states the claim, the
  voice explains it. Reading and hearing identical text wastes the second channel.
- Numbers and names belong in captions (they survive screenshots); nuance belongs in voice.
- Never put a URL, a handle or a call to action in the frame — the platform UI covers the bottom
  quarter and the file gets reused across three apps.

## Beat-sheet patterns that work for games

**Devlog clip**

| Scene | Caption | Narration |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | "week 14" | Two weeks ago this was a grey box. |
| 2 | "the water was fake" | The water did nothing. It was a blue plane. |
| 3 | "now it pushes you" | Now the current moves the player, the debris and the camera. |
| 4 | "same level, same code" | Same level. Same code path. One parameter. |
| 5 | "week 15?" | Next week: fish that react to the current. |

**Feature reveal**

| Scene | Caption | Narration |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | "you can now parry" | Parrying is in. |
| 2 | "0.2 s window" | The window is five frames. |
| 3 | "miss = stagger" | Miss it and you are open for a second. |
| 4 | "it's in the demo" | It ships in the demo on Friday. |

**Launch short**

| Scene | Caption | Narration |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | "it's out" | The game is out. |
| 2 | "2 years" | Two years, two people. |
| 3 | "19.99" | Nineteen ninety-nine, no DLC planned. |
| 4 | "link below" | Link is below. |

## Checklist before recording

- Every scene has exactly one sentence group to speak.
- Captions read in under 2.5 seconds at 2 lines.
- The first three seconds survive with sound off (captions only).
- The last line points back at the first.
- No scene needs two images — if it does, split the scene.
